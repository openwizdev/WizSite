# WizSite hill-climb · public brief (rubric v1, frozen for this stretch)

Shared by builders and judges. Synthetic and public: no private words, no evaluation state.

## Project taste brief
- Project and surface: openwiz.dev, the public landing page (`/`) and its ship log (`/changelog/`), phone 390 × 844 and desktop 1440 × 900 CSS px.
- Source revision: the OpenWiz mood-board summary (2026-11-02; dark only), refined by the landing-page grill of 2026-11-15 (direction option 1 chosen, then rounds 5–8 of that grill).
- Hard rules (every candidate keeps these):
  - Dark only. Navy, black and brass: ground near #07090f, navy engraving #12204a–#2b4a8f, brass #e8a849 as the one warm accent. White and grey text.
  - The engraved citadel and the little factory crew (builders, reviewers, the decider) are the identity. Calm, engraved, never goofy.
  - Headline "Build at the speed of ideas." and subline "You make the decisions. The agents run the factory." stay word for word.
  - Two calls to action, outside the animation: **Install · coming soon** (disabled until a stranger can install, with one short line) and **Star on GitHub**. Left of the animation on desktop, below it on the phone.
  - The hero intro plays once, then rests with ambient motion. Replay and Pause controls; one still frame under reduced motion or without JavaScript. Words appear in the animation only where a person would read them (the product window, spec labels).
  - The intro's story: one idea, a 3-question grill, a spec of 5 tickets with a plan picture, Approve, builders, a reviewer, merged; then one continuous pull-back where nearby lines light, each carrying a named spec item, then the whole lit citadel.
  - "How it works": OpenWiz runs your factory · WizSkills are the skills your agents follow · WizPlugins put the controls in Cloudroom, plus the fit sentence.
  - `/changelog` is the ship log: Being built (named bundles, never version numbers) and Shipped (published releases, notes unchanged, main line plus Also changed).
  - No invented claims, numbers, testimonials or logos. Never the word "play" + "book". No "Illustration, not live data" or "Building in public" notes. No new npm dependencies.
- Explicit preferences (paraphrased, owner, 2026-11-15): understand the offer and the main action in about 10 seconds; mobile must look good and is judged first; more motion at rest, but subtle and cheap; explain the repos in plain text; simplify and remove filler ("slop"); animations that read as a factory working, not labelled diagrams.
- Observed patterns (references, not requirements): best-in-class developer-tool pages lead with one sentence and one primary action, show the product early, keep a strong type scale, use few accents, and make the changelog scannable by date and headline.

## Marketing brief
- Audience: developers who run coding agents and want them to ship real work.
- Promise: you approve one plan; agents build, review and merge it, many ideas at once.
- Proof: none claimed beyond the published release notes and public repositories; the animation is an illustration of the workflow.
- One action: Star on GitHub (Install becomes the action once it's available).

## Rubric v1 (score each 1–10 with one short reason; judge the phone first)
1. **clarity** · within 10 seconds of the first screen, can a developer tell what OpenWiz is and what to do next?
2. **story** · does the hero animation tell its story clearly and move smoothly (judge the motion strip)?
3. **mobile** · is the 390 px page composed deliberately: readable type, order, crops, no cramped or empty regions?
4. **identity** · is the visual identity distinctive and coherent, with no generic filler ("slop")?
5. **copy** · is every line clear, specific, short and true?
6. **shiplog** · how useful is the changelog page to someone deciding whether the product is alive and what changed?
7. **weight** · how light and fast is the page, from the measured numbers given (bytes, requests, load timings)?
